Thesis

Understanding the growth and electronic properties of rare earth Kagome metals

Physics research project (PHS3350), Monash University, supervised by Dr. Mark Edmonds, November 2025

During my bachelor research project, conducted over a 15-week semester under the supervision of Dr. Mark Edmonds, I contributed an average of 15 hours per week to investigating the electronic properties of the rare-earth Kagome metal TbMn6Sn6. The project was assessed through two progress reports, a presentation, and a written thesis, for which I achieved an overall grade of 96/100, reflecting my thorough investigation and strong scientific communication skills. Kagome lattices are of interest for next-generation electronics due to their unique electronic properties; in particular, atomically thin TbMn6Sn6 is predicted to exhibit a flat band and heavy electrons, with potential applications in quantum computing and energy-efficient electronics.

My work involved growing thin-film samples of varying thickness using Molecular Beam Epitaxy, followed by analysis with X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy at the Australian Synchrotron. I processed and fitted spectra for the Tb, Mn, and Sn core levels, and identified uncharacterised peaks in the Tb and Sn spectra. Through a detailed literature review, I evaluated potential explanations and proposed avenues for further exploration. This project strengthened my ability to analyse complex data and draw informed, evidence-based conclusions even in the absence of prior literature.

Events

Oct. 2026

TEDxMünchen

Munich Urban Colab, Munich

A range of speakers talked about personal motivation and overcoming obstacles, and some showcased their futuristic companies. I was especially inspired by Alona Pammer, co-founder of Devanthro, who is building humanoid robots for aged care.

Two things stood out to me about her approach. First, the robots are designed to support people’s independence, guiding elderly people through everyday tasks rather than taking over completely. Second, because homes are so unpredictable, her robots can first be operated remotely in VR, learning the specific environment before they have to work on their own. I’m excited to follow her work.

I also heard about Starforge for the first time, a company working towards factory-built, modular fusion reactors that can be deployed as a fleet. I was very curious about their approach and timeline for viable fusion energy, but the founder was understandably tight-lipped.

The event location was incredible, and I was fortunate to meet someone who works in the colab space to give me a tour of the workspaces.
